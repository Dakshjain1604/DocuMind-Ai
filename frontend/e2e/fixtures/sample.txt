DocuMind Test Fixture: The Aurora Protocol

The Aurora Protocol is a fictional data-synchronization standard invented for
testing purposes. It was designed by the fictional Meridian Systems Working
Group in the fictional year 2031.

The Aurora Protocol's headline capability is delta-compression handoff, which
lets two nodes exchange only the changed portion of a dataset instead of the
whole dataset. This reduces bandwidth usage by up to ninety percent in the
Working Group's benchmark scenarios.

The protocol defines three roles: the Anchor, which holds the canonical copy
of the dataset; the Relay, which forwards delta-compression handoff packets
between anchors; and the Witness, which independently verifies that a handoff
completed without data loss.

A second capability, quorum rollback, allows a majority of Witnesses to force
any Anchor back to its last verified state if corruption is detected. Quorum
rollback requires at least three Witnesses to agree before it can execute.

The Meridian Systems Working Group published the Aurora Protocol specification
under the name "Aurora Protocol v1" and reports that ninety percent bandwidth
reduction as its central performance claim.
